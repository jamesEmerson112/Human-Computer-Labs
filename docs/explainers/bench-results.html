MELD Overnight Benchmarks

One NVIDIA A40 48 GB Runpod pod (Secure Cloud, EU-SE-1, 9 vCPUs), run on 2026-10-15. Every score is on MELD's test split of 2,610 lines. Click any column heading to sort by it.

Our choices so far

  • Track: text plus vision, chosen on 2026-10-15.
  • Speech to text: Whisper large-v3-turbo in int8. Nemotron was about three times faster, but Whisper keeps the punctuation that carries emotion. On 30 test clips Whisper kept 11 of MELD's 23 exclamation marks, and Nemotron kept none.
  • Text encoder: EmbeddingGemma 300M by default. EmoBERTa-large is only a backup demo, because its scores may not be fair.
  • Reply writer: Liquid AI's LFM2.5 1.2B Instruct, the fastest below at 122 ms per reply.
  • Vision encoder: not chosen yet; a second A40 run is testing them.

Speech to text

Word error rate is measured against MELD's own text after Whisper's English normaliser. Many clips also hold words from the line before or after, which no model can avoid, so the absolute rates are high. Compare the rows with each other. The saved MELD.Whisper test transcripts score 31.80% the same way, which matches the int8 row.

Times are for one clip per call with the audio already decoded. GPU memory for the NeMo models (Nemotron and Parakeet) includes PyTorch's cached pool, so it overstates what the model needs.

Frozen encoders with a small head

Each encoder turns a line into numbers without being trained. Only the small head on top is trained on MELD: a logistic regression or a small two-layer network (MLP). Scores are weighted F1. The heads read one line at a time with no dialogue context.

Text plus voice

Raw and Completed are trained on MELD's published text and on our fixed text, and both are tested on MELD's published test text. Whisper is trained and tested on Whisper's transcripts, which is what a live demo would read.

EmoBERTa-large's released weights have likely seen MELD's test lines. It scores 77.9 here against 63.46 on its own model card, so its rows are struck through and never marked best. The text plus voice runs pair the voice encoders with EmbeddingGemma, the best text encoder on dev once EmoBERTa was left out.

Reply writers

Hugging Face transformers generate in bf16 at batch size 1, greedy, up to 40 new tokens, over 50 MELD test lines with the gold emotion in the prompt. Reasoning models ran with thinking turned off.

Nemotron 3 Nano, the Qwen3.5 family and LFM2.5 use Mamba, gated delta-rule or short-convolution layers, and they ran on transformers' slow reference kernels because causal-conv1d, mamba-ssm and flash-linear-attention were not installed. Their speeds here understate what they reach with those kernels or with vLLM.

Parameters loaded counts only the part that writes text. For Qwen3.5-4B (4.66B in the catalog), Qwen3.5-2B (2.27B) and Gemma 4 E2B it leaves out the vision and audio parts that the 6B limit counts.

Zero-shot vision-language models

No MELD training. Each model saw 8 frames from the clip, once alone and once with the spoken line, and picked one of MELD's seven labels.

The prediction is the label whose first token the model scores highest as the start of its answer, from one forward pass, so every answer is a label. A first try that let models write freely was thrown away, because Qwen3.5-4B opened 1,579 of 1,585 answers with an explanation instead of a label.

Each model had a 40-minute limit, so a row may cover a random subset of the 2,457 clips whose frames extracted. Some labels' first tokens are short fragments that can also start other words, so those labels may be picked slightly too often. OneEmo's paper feeds 16 frames with its own prompt and reports a MELD hit rate, which is not weighted F1, so its lower score here is not a direct contradiction of that paper.

About the run

The pod ran from 08:58 to 16:21 UTC, 7.4 hours. At $0.59 an hour that is about $4.35 of GPU time. Runpod's billing showed $3.93 when the pod was stopped, and its figures can lag. The pod is stopped with its 150 GB volume kept, which bills about $1 a day.

Frames failed to extract for 153 of the 2,610 test clips. The source tables are in ML_route/bench/results/README.md.
