Weighted F1 averages each emotion's F1 score, weighting
      each emotion by how many lines carry that label. MELD papers report it on

With serve.py, serve_emotion.py and
    serve_reply.py running, every line gets three model outputs when
    its clip ends. Whisper transcribes the clip's audio. The trained emotion head
    reads the clip's sound with HuBERT and its words with EmbeddingGemma and
    EmoBERTa. The words are the live Whisper transcript when live speech-to-text
    is on, and the saved Whisper transcript otherwise. LFM2.5 then writes a reply
    that answers the head's emotion and words. By default the reply also sees the
    dialogue's earlier lines, and the menu beside Live reply switches it to the
    line alone. The grey tag is MELD's own label, and the head's emotion turns
    green when the two agree. The time beside it is how long the emotion took
    after the clip ended. The models take turns on the one GPU, so later ones
    wait. A random emotion, marked random, appears only when Live emotion is off
    or its server is down, and the reply then answers MELD's own label. A canned
    reply, marked Placeholder reply, appears only when Live reply is off or its
    server is down. The head trained on the train split and was picked by its
    dev score, so the page is meant for the test split.
