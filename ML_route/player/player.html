Weighted F1 averages each emotion's F1 score, weighting
      each emotion by how many lines carry that label. MELD papers report it on
      the test split, times 100. The best published text and audio result in

Lines

      

        
        
      

    

    
Each line gets a random emotion and a random reply for that
    emotion when its clip ends. The grey
    tag is MELD's own label, and the guess turns green when the two agree. The
    time beside the guess is how long the emotion took after the clip ended.
    With serve.py running, live speech-to-text transcribes each
    clip's audio on the GPU when the clip ends and shows how long that took.
    Whisper runs the speech-to-text. With serve_reply.py up, LFM2.5 also writes two live
    replies to each line for MELD's own emotion label, one from the line alone and
    one from the dialogue so far, compared in the table below. The models take turns on the GPU, so
    later ones wait.

    

  

  

    
Replies compared

    
LFM2.5 writes two replies to each line of this dialogue. The
    first sees only that line. The second also sees every earlier line of the
    dialogue as a plain transcript, without its own earlier replies. Both answer
    MELD's own emotion label for the line, not the random guess, so the replies
    can be judged on their own.
